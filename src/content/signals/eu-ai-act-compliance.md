## Industry Background

The EU AI Act, formally adopted in March 2024, is the world's first comprehensive legal framework for artificial intelligence, categorizing AI systems into risk tiers from minimal to unacceptable. Compliance deadlines are staggered, with high-risk systems facing obligations by mid-2025 and general-purpose AI models by early 2026. The regulation imposes stringent requirements for transparency, human oversight, and risk management, with fines reaching up to 7% of global annual turnover or EUR 35 million, whichever is higher. This creates an immediate compliance imperative for any company deploying AI within the EU, including non-EU firms whose AI outputs affect European citizens. The market forces at play are threefold: first, the sheer complexity of the Act, which runs over 450 pages and references dozens of technical standards, overwhelms most internal legal teams; second, the lack of harmonized enforcement mechanisms across member states means companies must prepare for multiple interpretations; third, the rapid pace of AI development, especially generative AI, introduces new compliance risks daily. Existing governance tools are either too generic (e.g., general risk management software) or too expensive (e.g., custom legal consulting), leaving a gap for scalable, domain-specific solutions. The industry background is characterized by a race against time, with many organizations only now realizing that compliance is not optional but a prerequisite for market access. This regulatory tailwind is further amplified by the EU's Digital Services Act and GDPR, creating a layered compliance burden that demands integrated tooling.
