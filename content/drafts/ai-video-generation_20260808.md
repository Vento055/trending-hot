## Mechanism Analysis: Why AI Video Generation Is Surging

To understand the rise of **AI video generation**, we must examine the underlying technological and economic mechanisms driving adoption.

### The Diffusion Transformer Breakthrough

The core driver is the shift from GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks) to **Diffusion Transformers (DiTs)** . This architectural change, popularized by OpenAI's Sora in early 2024, allowed models to process video data in patches—similar to how large language models process text tokens. This enables generation of longer, more coherent sequences.

### The Compute Cost Curve

Video generation is computationally expensive. A single 10-second clip can require **thousands of GPU hours** for training and significant inference costs. However, the mechanism of **progressive distillation**—whereby a large, slow model teaches a smaller, faster one—has cut inference costs by **70-80%** in the last year. This is why prices have fallen while quality has risen.

### Open-Source vs. Closed-Source Dynamics

The mechanism of competition between open-source models (like Stable Video Diffusion) and closed-source platforms (like Google Veo) has accelerated innovation. Open-source releases act as a floor for quality, forcing commercial players to continuously improve. This dynamic is visible in the rapid iteration from Veo 1 to Veo 3 in just 12 months.

### The Data Flywheel

Every user prompt and generated video becomes a training data point (when opted in). This creates a **flywheel effect** where more users produce better outputs, which attract more users. This explains why platforms like Kling and Runway are aggressively subsidizing free tiers—they are buying data, not just market share.
