The resurgence is driven by **Studio Pierrot's decision to adapt the final arc with a seasonal format**, similar to *Attack on Titan*. ### The Nostalgia Economy
The primary demographic watching this new arc are the adults who grew up with the series in the mid-2000s. This demographic now has disposable income. They are the ones subscribing to newsletters, buying premium merchandise, and engaging in online discourse. The mechanism here is **nostalgic capital**—the conversion of childhood memories into adult spending.

### The "Canon Divergence" Hook
One of the most significant drivers of newsletter sign-ups is the fact that the anime is **expanding on the manga**. Tite Kubo is adding new scenes, fights, and character backgrounds that were not in the original manga. This creates a "Fear Of Missing Out" (FOMO) mechanism. Fans *need* the newsletter to stay updated on what is canon and what is new, making the newsletter an essential utility rather than a luxury.

### The Streaming Algorithm
Platforms like Hulu and Disney+ (which host the series internationally) have algorithms that favor consistent engagement. The stable Google Trends data (60) suggests that the show is being discovered by new audiences daily, not just during premiere weeks. This algorithmic push keeps the series in the public consciousness, fueling the need for constant updates that newsletters provide.
